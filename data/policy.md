## Application

This policy applies to UnitedHealthcare Commercial and Individual Exchange benefit plans.

Pretest genetic counseling is strongly recommended so the individual understands the
benefits, limitations, and possible outcomes of testing.

---

## Coverage Rationale

### BRCA1 and BRCA2 Testing

BRCA1/2 testing is proven and medically necessary for an individual with a personal history
of breast cancer diagnosed at age 65 or younger.

### Multigene Hereditary Cancer Panels

A multigene hereditary cancer panel is proven and medically necessary for an individual with
a personal history of a primary solid tumor, other than basal or squamous cell skin cancer,
when at least one qualifying pathway below is met.

#### Personal cancer history

The individual's cancer history includes one or more of the following:

- Breast cancer diagnosed at age 50 or younger
- Metastatic breast cancer
- More than one primary breast cancer, including bilateral disease
- Triple-negative breast cancer
- Lobular breast cancer together with a personal or family history of diffuse gastric cancer
- Breast cancer in an individual assigned male at birth
- Breast cancer in an individual of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry
- Breast or prostate cancer plus a first- or second-degree relative with a BRCA-related cancer
- Ovarian, fallopian-tube, or primary peritoneal cancer
- Pancreatic cancer
- Metastatic prostate cancer
- A Lynch syndrome-associated cancer
- A neuroendocrine tumor such as adrenocortical carcinoma, paraganglioma, or pheochromocytoma
- Two or more different primary solid tumors

#### Personal and family cancer history

An individual with a primary solid tumor may also qualify based on family history, including
one or more of the following:

- A close blood relative with a Lynch syndrome-associated cancer
- A first- or second-degree relative diagnosed with a primary solid tumor at age 40 or younger
- At least two additional close blood relatives on the same side of the family with primary
  solid tumors

#### Tumor or risk-assessment findings

An individual with a primary solid tumor may also qualify when:

- Tumor testing identifies a pathogenic variant with potential germline significance
- Tumor testing is microsatellite-instability high or immunohistochemistry shows loss of one
  or more mismatch-repair proteins
- An accepted risk model meets the policy threshold for a BRCA1/2 or Lynch syndrome variant

### Not Medically Necessary

Multigene hereditary cancer panels are unproven and not medically necessary when none of the
listed indications is met. The following are also unproven or not medically necessary under
this policy:

- RNA-only hereditary cancer panels
- Testing performed only to generate a polygenic risk score
- Whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing to identify hereditary cancer risk

## Medical Records Documentation Used for Reviews

Records submitted for review should support the coverage pathway being used. Depending on the
case, relevant documentation may include:

- The cancer diagnosis and age at diagnosis
- Pathology and tumor biomarker results, including ER, PR, and HER2 status when breast cancer
  subtype is relevant
- Personal and three-generation family cancer history
- Prior germline or tumor genetic testing
- The ordered test, genes or conditions included, and testing methodology
- How the result may affect treatment, surgery, surveillance, or risk assessment
- Evidence that genetic counseling was offered or provided

## Definitions

**BRCA-Related Cancers:** Breast, ovarian, fallopian-tube, primary peritoneal, pancreatic, and
prostate cancers.

**Close Blood Relative:** A first-, second-, or third-degree relative on the same side of the
family.

**First-Degree Relative:** A parent, sibling, or child.

**Multigene Hereditary Cancer Panel:** A next-generation sequencing test that evaluates
multiple genes associated with inherited cancer susceptibility.

**Primary Solid Tumor:** The original solid-tumor cancer arising in the body, rather than a
metastasis from another primary site.

**Triple-Negative Breast Cancer:** Breast cancer that is negative for estrogen receptor,
progesterone receptor, and HER2 expression or amplification.
